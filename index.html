OpenAI's reasoning model autonomously disproves the Erdős unit distance conjecture – an 80-year-old open problem in discrete mathematics

On 20 May 2026, OpenAI announced that one of its internal general-purpose reasoning models autonomously disproved the Erdős unit distance conjecture, a central open problem in discrete geometry first posed by mathematician Paul Erdős in 1946. The proof – 125 pages – establishes an infinite family of planar point configurations that produce significantly more unit-distance pairs than the square-grid arrangements mathematicians had assumed to be optimal for nearly eight decades. Fields medalist Tim Gowers reviewed the proof independently and described it as "a milestone in AI mathematics." Princeton mathematician Will Sawin refined the result and quantified the improvement: the best configurations now scale as n^(1+δ), where δ ≥ 0.014, confirmed in a companion paper published the same day.

The method surprised the mathematical community most. Rather than extending known geometry techniques, the model connected the problem to algebraic number theory – specifically, Golod-Shafarevich class field towers – a deep branch of mathematics with no prior connection to combinatorial geometry. Number theorist Arul Shankar stated the work shows AI systems can move beyond assisting mathematicians to generating genuinely original ideas. OpenAI confirmed the model was not specifically trained on the unit distance problem and received only the problem statement before producing the proof independently.

Why it mattersThis is the clearest demonstration to date that AI reasoning systems can operate as independent mathematical contributors, not just accelerants of existing human effort. Fields that have long-standing unsolved problems – physics, biology, cryptography, drug design – must now seriously evaluate whether AI can serve as a primary research agent, not merely a literature assistant. The 80-year gap between a problem being posed and its resolution collapsing to an AI-generated proof is a structural shift in how scientific discovery timelines should be modeled.

Anthropic co-founder Jack Clark assigns 60%+ probability to AI training its own successor by end of 2028 – describes it as "intelligence explosion"

Anthropic co-founder Jack Clark delivered the 2026 Cosmos Lecture at the University of Oxford on 20 May 2026, titled "Change is inevitable. Autonomy is not." He made four timed predictions: AI will collaborate with humans on a Nobel Prize-winning discovery within 12 months; bipedal robots will assist tradespeople within 2 years; companies run entirely by AI will generate millions of dollars in revenue within 18 months; and – most significantly – there is a 60%+ probability that by the end of 2028 an AI model will be able to fully train its successor, a process called recursive self-improvement. Clark used the phrase "intelligence explosion" – long confined to AI safety theory – and confirmed it now appears in official Anthropic research documents. He also stated there is a "non-zero chance" AI could kill everyone on the planet, and that this risk "hasn't gone away."

The Anthropic Institute, Clark's research division on the societal consequences of advanced AI, published a five-page research agenda this week explicitly referencing the intelligence explosion framing. Clark's 60% estimate is not a detached forecast; it describes the trajectory of work Anthropic is actively pursuing, particularly following the company's hiring of Andrej Karpathy specifically to use Claude to accelerate pretraining research – which is, in practice, early-stage recursive self-improvement.

Why it mattersWhen a co-founder of one of the three most capable AI labs publicly assigns a majority probability to recursive self-improvement occurring within 30 months – and that assessment is backed by an official published research agenda – it is no longer a fringe risk scenario. Governments, insurers, infrastructure operators, and financial regulators have no established frameworks for an environment where AI development timelines are set by AI systems rather than human engineering schedules. The policy and institutional lag is now measurable in years, not decades.

Leaked audio: Meta tracked employee Gmail, coding activity, and internal tools to train AI – on the same day 8,000 were fired

A leaked audio recording from a Meta all-hands meeting on 30 April 2026, published by labor-focused outlet More Perfect Union on 19 May, surfaced publicly on the same day approximately 8,000 Meta employees received layoff notices. In the audio, CEO Mark Zuckerberg describes a programme called the Model Capability Initiative (MCI): an internal effort that monitored employee activity on Gmail, Google Chat, the internal assistant Metamate, and VS Code to train Meta's AI models on how highly capable people work. "The AI models learn from watching really smart people do things," Zuckerberg says in the recording. He added: "No human is looking at or watching what people are doing on their computers."

The reaction across Meta's workforce was immediate and organized. Employees pasted protest flyers inside meeting rooms and circulated a petition against MCI. The consent and transparency question here is not whether the tracking was technically anonymous – Zuckerberg's assurance on that point may be accurate. The question is whether harvesting work patterns without explicit, informed consent for a purpose directly connected to reducing the roles of the people being monitored is ethically defensible, irrespective of anonymization. No independent verification of Meta's anonymization claims has been published.

Why it mattersThis is the first confirmed case of a major technology company systematically using its own employees' work data – collected without explicit consent for this purpose – to train the AI systems that subsequently replaced them. The structural precedent is significant: if MCI is not actionable under current US labor law, it signals that consent frameworks for employee data use in AI training are absent. Every large enterprise currently building internal AI on workforce data should audit its consent architecture immediately.

GitHub loses 3,800 internal repositories to TeamPCP via poisoned Nx Console VS Code extension – active for 18 minutes

GitHub confirmed on 21 May 2026 that approximately 3,800 internal repositories were exfiltrated by threat actor group TeamPCP (also tracked as UNC6780) following the compromise of an employee device. The attack vector was a trojanized version of the Nx Console VS Code extension – which has 2.2 million installs and verified publisher status – that was live on the Visual Studio Marketplace for exactly 18 minutes, between 12:30 PM and 12:48 PM UTC on 18 May 2026. During that window, the malicious extension ran silently on startup, executing a shell command disguised as a routine MCP setup task that downloaded a credential stealer from a planted commit in the official nrwl/nx GitHub repository. The stealer targeted 1Password vaults, Anthropic Claude Code configuration files (at ~/.claude/settings.json), npm tokens, GitHub personal access tokens, and AWS credentials.

GitHub CISO Alexis Wales confirmed: "We have no evidence of impact to customer information stored outside of GitHub's internal repositories." The 18-minute window between a trojanized extension being published and removed is the defining operational fact of this incident. Standard endpoint security does not monitor VS Code extension installs in real time. Auto-update, enabled by default across VS Code, gives any publisher with a compromised release pipeline a direct push channel to every developer machine – with no marketplace review gate or waiting period.

Why it mattersThis breach demonstrates that the fastest measured developer supply chain attack-to-credential harvest cycle is now 18 minutes – shorter than most security team response protocols. The attack surface is not perimeter-based; it is the trusted package and extension ecosystem that every developer uses daily. Any organisation running Claude Code, Claude Cowork, or other Anthropic tooling alongside Nx Console on developer machines should treat credentials harvested in that window as compromised until rotated.

TeamPCP's Mini Shai-Hulud worm hits OpenAI, Mistral, European Commission, and 170+ npm packages – CVE score 9.6

The GitHub breach is one confirmed stop on a broader campaign. TeamPCP's Mini Shai-Hulud worm (Wave 4) launched on 11 May 2026 by compromising TanStack's entire router ecosystem, spreading across 170+ npm packages and two PyPI packages in a single coordinated campaign. The associated CVE, CVE-2026-45321, carries a CVSS score of 9.6. Confirmed victims beyond GitHub include: OpenAI (two employee devices compromised; limited credential material exfiltrated from internal source code repositories; iOS, macOS, and Windows code-signing certificates rotated); Mistral AI (one developer device compromised, facing a $25,000 Monero extortion demand for an alleged 5 GB source code leak); the European Commission's public website; and data contracting firm Mercor. Trend Micro has tracked at least seven confirmed TeamPCP waves in 2026 alone, with prior targets including Aqua's Trivy security scanner, Checkmarx KICS, LiteLLM, and Bitwarden CLI.

OpenAI confirmed the certificate rotation timeline publicly: iOS and Windows certificates have already been rotated; the macOS ChatGPT desktop application code-signing certificate will be fully revoked on 12 June 2026. This means the current macOS ChatGPT desktop app will become untrusted by macOS Gatekeeper after that date – users will need to reinstall the application from a freshly signed build. OpenAI has not yet issued a public notification to end users about this requirement. TeamPCP is building a self-sustaining attack loop: compromise one trusted developer tool, harvest credentials, use those credentials to access the next trusted tool or repository, repeat.

Why it mattersTeamPCP is demonstrating an industrialized exploitation pattern targeting AI-adjacent developer tooling systematically. With OpenAI, Mistral, GitHub, and the European Commission all confirmed victims within a two-week window, the AI developer tooling supply chain must be treated as an active threat environment – not a theoretical one. The loop is running faster than standard security patching cycles.

Anthropic acquires Stainless to own the full SDK and MCP server layer around the Claude API

Anthropic confirmed on 18 May 2026 that it has acquired Stainless, a startup founded in 2022 that builds high-quality software development kits for API products. We're excited to bring the Stainless team into Anthropic to advance Claude's ability to connect to data and tools." The acquisition brings in-house the team responsible for how developers experience the Claude API at the code level – including CLIs and MCP server tooling. By acquiring the leading SDK generation tooling for MCP servers, Anthropic now controls both the protocol and the primary implementation layer. No acquisition price was disclosed. For developers who have observed the gap in ergonomics between Claude's SDKs and OpenAI's SDKs, that gap is now being closed deliberately and in-house.

For enterprise buyers evaluating Claude vs. competing platforms, SDK quality is a real adoption barrier; this acquisition addresses it structurally.
