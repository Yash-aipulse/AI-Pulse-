Nvidia reports $115.8 billion in quarterly revenue, up 103% from a year ago, and forecasts about $130 billion for the next quarter while assuming no data center compute sales to China

Nvidia published results on 17 November 2026 for its fiscal third quarter, which ended on 25 October. Revenue came to $115.8 billion, 20% higher than the previous quarter and 103% higher than a year earlier. Its data center business brought in $107.6 billion of that total, up 110% year on year, which by our calculation is about 93% of all revenue. GAAP net income was $65.3 billion and GAAP gross margin was 74.8%. Colette Kress, who is serving as interim CEO while remaining chief financial officer, is the executive quoted in the release, and she said its newest rack-scale system, Vera Rubin NVL72, is "off to an explosive start."

For the fourth quarter, which includes an extra week, Nvidia expects revenue of $130.0 billion, plus or minus 3%, with gross margins of 71.0% to 72.0%, below this quarter's level. The company says its forecast does not count on any data center compute revenue from China. It returned $28.1 billion to shareholders during the quarter through buybacks and dividends. The release also says Vera Rubin NVL72 racks are already running at CoreWeave, Google Cloud, Microsoft Azure, Oracle Cloud Infrastructure and Nebius, and lists Nvidia's agreement to acquire Hugging Face among the quarter's announcements.

Why it mattersNvidia's revenue is the clearest single reading of how much the world is spending on AI infrastructure, and this quarter shows that spending still rising quickly despite a leadership change at the top of the company. The forecast also tells buyers that the next generation of hardware is moving into cloud data centers now, while Nvidia is planning on the basis that China stays closed to its data center chips.

Financial Services Policy & Regulation

The SEC is opening a civil investigation into whether Nvidia's disclosures misled investors, The Wall Street Journal reports, adding to pressure on the company after Jensen Huang's resignation

The US Securities and Exchange Commission is opening a civil investigation into whether Nvidia's disclosures misled investors, according to The Wall Street Journal, in a report carried by TechCrunch on 17 November 2026. TechCrunch's account does not say which disclosures are under review, and it includes no comment from either Nvidia or the SEC. The report came on the same day Nvidia announced third-quarter revenue of $115.8 billion, up 103% from a year earlier.

The probe adds to the scrutiny Nvidia has faced since Jensen Huang stepped down as CEO following reports of extramarital affairs, one of them with an executive at Megaspeed, a data company based in Singapore. According to TechCrunch, the Commerce Department has been investigating Megaspeed since late 2024 on suspicion that it diverted Nvidia chips to China in breach of US export law. Congress has pressed Huang to explain whether the relationship influenced his support for easing export restrictions, and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has accused Nvidia of knowingly selling powerful chips to China. TechCrunch says Nvidia's shares have dropped more than 10% since Huang's departure.

Why it mattersA securities investigation moves the questions about Nvidia's China sales from export politics into the accuracy of what the company tells investors. Because so much AI infrastructure spending flows through Nvidia, any finding that it misstated risks or where its chips ended up would affect how investors, auditors and customers read the numbers behind the AI buildout.

Anthropic is set to price its IPO at or above the top of its $70 to $78 range after orders reached close to seven times the shares on offer, Bloomberg and The Wall Street Journal report

Anthropic, which is marketing its shares at $70 to $78 each, plans to price them at or above the top of that range after drawing orders for close to seven times the shares available, Bloomberg reported on Tuesday, 17 November 2026, citing unnamed sources. The Wall Street Journal, also citing unnamed sources, reported that the price could go above $80 and that the company's advisers have discussed valuations of roughly $1.5 trillion or $1.6 trillion. According to PYMNTS, which summarized both reports, banks were expected to stop taking orders at 4 p.m. New York time on Tuesday, pricing is set for Wednesday, 18 November, and trading is expected to start on Thursday, 19 November. Bloomberg cautioned that details could still change.

Anthropic's 10 November filing, as reported by PYMNTS, set a target valuation of about $1.4 trillion and aimed to raise as much as $90.4 billion, made up of a $64.4 billion base offering and a $26 billion private placement. Bloomberg says that at $78 a share the offering would raise $90.4 billion at a fully diluted valuation of $1.4 trillion. The strong order book is a shift from early November, when the Journal reported that three major planned investors doubted the company would reach a $1.5 trillion valuation after a tech selloff, with one putting it closer to $1 trillion. Anthropic did not immediately respond to PYMNTS' request for comment.

Why it mattersThe final price will become a public reference point for how markets value companies that build frontier AI models, and it will feed straight into private funding rounds, vendor negotiations and AI-heavy stock valuations. Once listed, Anthropic will also have to report results every quarter, which should give enterprise customers far more visibility into the finances of a supplier many of them now depend on.

Operations & Enterprise

Microsoft makes Copilot the front door to Dynamics 365 at Ignite, launches a tool for moving CRM customers off Salesforce, and says an internal study cut seller effort by more than 25%

At its Ignite conference in San Francisco on Tuesday, 17 November 2026, Microsoft said Copilot will now act as the main entry point to Dynamics 365, its CRM and ERP software, according to a blog post by Jeff Teper, executive vice president for Microsoft Apps and Agents. The company is packaging job-specific abilities into plugins. Plugins for sales and customer service are now generally available and together carry more than 57 business skills, while a plugin for ERP apps adds more than 39 finance and operations skills along with wider links to Excel. Microsoft says agent accounts will enter public preview in December 2026, and that cost-management tools for its Agent 365 product are now in the Microsoft 365 admin center, with spending forecasts arriving through its Frontier program this month.

Microsoft is also going after rivals' customers. A service called Dynamics 365 Activate for CRM, built to help companies migrate from Salesforce, is generally available, and a version for ERP, starting with SAP ECC, is in private preview. Partner congruentX says one large enterprise client is on track to move 20,000 users off Salesforce in about five months. In an internal Microsoft sales study, the company reports more than a 25% drop in seller effort on account planning, meeting preparation and proposals, and a 9% improvement in how quickly deals close. Microsoft notes these figures come from its own telemetry and evaluations, and Visa is named as running a focused pilot of Copilot for sales.

Why it mattersMicrosoft is turning its business applications into a set of skills that sit behind a single AI assistant, and it is pairing that with a migration service aimed squarely at Salesforce and SAP customers. For enterprise buyers, the next CRM or ERP renewal is likely to become a negotiation about AI agents, migration costs and how agent usage is billed, not only about seats and features.

Financial Services Policy & Regulation

Singapore sets three national AI missions for financial services, including an industry-wide scam detection study from early 2027 and a sandbox for payments made by AI agents

Singapore's Deputy Prime Minister Gan Kim Yong, who also chairs the Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS), used the Singapore FinTech Festival on 18 November 2026 to set out three national AI missions for the financial sector, Fintech News Singapore reports: fighting financial crime, widening access to finance, especially for small and medium-sized businesses, and letting AI agents carry out transactions safely. On fraud, MAS will start a study in early 2027 to design a shared Scam Detection Utility for the whole industry. It builds on a trial, first announced in May, in which five banks pool transaction data securely to test whether AI can spot patterns that no single bank could see. The utility would start with accounts and data in Singapore. "Scams are not confined within the boundary of one bank," Gan said.

MAS and the Future of Finance Institute will also open a synthetic dataset, built with the Government Technology Agency and modeled on the five-bank trial's interbank and scam patterns without sharing the original records, to developers through an AI Innovation Arena. On agent payments, the institute will set up an Agentic Money Sandbox where financial institutions test how AI agents transact with digital money, and which safeguards are needed, before any live use, Fintech News Singapore reports separately. The first use cases are payments started by AI agents and treasury management, and the trials will look at how to verify an agent's identity and what it is allowed to do.

Why it mattersA major financial regulator is moving from guidance to shared infrastructure for AI: pooled fraud data across banks and a supervised test bed for agents that move money. Banks, payment firms and corporate treasuries in Asia now have a concrete route for testing agent payments with a regulator in the room, and an early view of the controls that are likely to be expected elsewhere.

London-based Model ML raises $250 million at a $1.5 billion valuation for AI agents that build financial models and pitch decks for bankers, TechCrunch reports

Model ML, a three-year-old start-up based in London, has raised a $250 million Series B at a $1.5 billion valuation, TechCrunch reported on 17 November 2026. General Atlantic and earlier investor QED Investors co-led the round, which comes a year after the company announced a $75 million Series A. Its software takes on tasks usually given to junior bankers and consultants, such as building financial models, putting together pitch decks and drafting due diligence reports. The company was founded by brothers Chaz and Arnie Englander.

The agents plug into a client's own systems, data and tools, including Excel and PowerPoint. Model ML says they keep track of the full context of a deal and can refresh models and presentations on their own when new developments change the analysis. The company also says it runs on open-source models, which it claims lets it operate at about 2% of the cost of frontier models while producing results faster. TechCrunch names Hebbia and Rogo as competitors, noting that Rogo raised $160 million at a $2 billion valuation in April, and that Anthropic and OpenAI have built tools for finance professionals as well.

Why it mattersInvestors are now paying unicorn prices for AI that does the core analyst work of banking and consulting, not just research or search. That puts pressure on how advisory firms staff deals and price their work, and the open-source cost claim, if it holds up in practice, could change what firms expect to pay for this kind of software.

Giga Energy signs a 10-year lease worth about $1 billion with an unnamed US AI cloud provider for a 60-megawatt data center in Texas, Reuters reports

Giga Energy has agreed a 10-year lease worth about $1 billion with a US-based AI cloud provider that it did not name, Reuters reported on Tuesday, 17 November 2026. The deal covers a 60-megawatt data center under development in Hunt County, Texas, known as Project Clay. Giga Energy will build, own and run the campus for its customer.

According to Reuters, ground was broken in July and the site is expected to start operating in spring 2027. The company plans to use factory-built construction, which it expects to complete in under eight months, and says the facility will use a closed-loop cooling system that does not draw on municipal water or groundwater. Reuters did not report how the project is being financed.

Why it mattersLong leases with AI cloud providers are becoming the way new data center capacity gets funded, and factory-built designs are shrinking build times to months. The closed-loop cooling design is also worth noting for buyers who track the water use of the sites they rely on. For energy and infrastructure planners, smaller, faster sites like this one add steady new demand in regions such as Texas.

Indeed and Glassdoor find the share of job postings that mention AI doubled to 7.2% in a year, yet 56% of US workers surveyed do not agree that AI makes them more efficient

Indeed Hiring Lab and Glassdoor Economic Research published new findings on AI and work on 18 November 2026, drawn from their 2027 US Hiring and Worklife trends report. Indeed's AI Tracker, which measures the share of its job postings that use AI terms, reached 7.2% in September 2026, twice the 3.6% of September 2025, and the share of companies with at least one posting that mentions AI rose 44% over the same period. In Indeed's 2026 Workforce Insights Report survey, regular use of AI at work rose from 36% to 42%. The authors say there is so far "scant evidence" of an AI-driven wave of job losses, but that AI is already changing roles and attitudes.

The payoff workers report is less clear. A majority of employed US respondents, 56%, did not agree that AI makes or could make them more efficient, and the daily time savings that regular users credit to AI slipped from 2.3 hours to 2.1 hours over the past year. On Glassdoor, 82% of reviews that mentioned AI in 2019 described it positively, against 45% in 2026, and 51% of reviews that mention AI now do so only in the "cons" section. Reviewers critical of AI were 7.1 times as likely as the base rate to mention layoffs. Hiring is also shifting toward physical infrastructure: a quarter of data center-related postings in the first half of 2026 were for installation and maintenance workers, with advertised wages 42% higher than for similar work outside data centers.

Why it mattersAI is spreading through hiring and daily work faster than workers see a payoff, and attitudes are turning more negative, often alongside worries about layoffs. For employers, that gap is a cost risk (paying for tools that staff do not find useful) and a people risk (falling trust in leadership while AI is being rolled out). It also gives executives a public benchmark to compare against their own adoption and sentiment data.

The money behind AI is still accelerating, and the fine print on control, cost and safeguards is becoming the real story.

Nvidia's $115.8 billion quarter, with $107.6 billion from data centers and a forecast of about $130 billion for the next three months, shows that AI infrastructure spending is still rising even as the company runs under an interim CEO and plans without data center sales to China, though a reported SEC probe into whether its disclosures misled investors shows how closely its account of China sales is now being checked. For executives, the practical steps are to plan AI budgets for tight compute supply rather than falling prices, check export-control records with chip suppliers, refresh AI valuation benchmarks once Anthropic prices, test finance agents on closed deals before live ones, define limits and approvals before letting agents move money, treat agent billing and migration terms as part of every software renewal, and measure whether staff actually save time with the AI tools they are given.
