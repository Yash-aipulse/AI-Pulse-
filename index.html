Financial Services Hardware & Devices

AI stocks give back ground a day after Jensen Huang's return, with Nvidia down 5.9% and Anthropic slipping below its $83 IPO price in early Tuesday trade, AP reports

Monday's relief rally did not last. In its Tuesday morning market report, the Associated Press said that shortly after the open the S&P 500 was down 1.1% and on course for its eighth loss in nine sessions, the Nasdaq composite was down 1.6% as of 9:35 a.m. Eastern time, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average was 118 points, or 0.2%, lower. Nvidia fell 5.9% after jumping 31.5% on Monday, which AP describes as its largest one-day gain in more than two decades. Other AI names dropped too: AP reports CoreWeave down 7.1% and GE Vernova down 3.9%.

Anthropic, which went public last week, fell 8.2% and traded under $83, the price at which it sold shares in its IPO; on its first day it briefly reached $115, according to AP. The agency lists several reasons for the renewed selling: worry that AI share prices rose too far and may need to deflate, doubts that AI will earn enough to justify data center spending, high bond yields that AP links partly to the war with Iran, and concern that Democratic midterm gains could bring tighter AI rules, possibly including pauses on data center construction. AP notes Huang's return does not resolve those questions. These are early-session figures and may differ from closing levels.

Why it mattersA one-day bounce on a leadership change has not repaired confidence in AI valuations. With the newest listed AI developer already below its offer price within a week, boards and finance teams should assume that capital for AI projects will be priced more carefully, and that investors will keep asking for proof of returns on data center spending rather than promises.

Attorney General Todd Blanche vouches for Nvidia as a "good corporate citizen" but stops short of clearing Jensen Huang, Politico reports

At a Justice Department press conference on Tuesday, Attorney General Todd Blanche publicly backed Nvidia, describing the chipmaker as a "good corporate citizen," according to Politico. He declined to discuss his conversations with President Trump about the company and argued that speculating about Huang was unfair both to the process and to Huang himself. FBI Director Kash Patel, who also attended, declined to go into the Nvidia and Huang questions, Politico reports.

Politico notes that Blanche did not go as far as saying Huang was free of any fault, and that reporters reminded him that on 12 November he had said the FBI was looking into allegations about Huang. The remarks came a day after Trump, in an interview with CNBC, said a Justice Department inquiry had cleared Huang of claims that he helped divert chips from Singapore to China. According to Politico, lawmakers in both parties are uneasy about Trump's role in Huang's return, and Huang is expected to testify next week, in public and in private, before House and Senate committees.

Why it mattersThe top law enforcement official is signaling support for Nvidia without formally closing the questions around its chief executive. That leaves a live political and legal overhang on the world's most important AI chip supplier, with congressional hearings next week as the next test. Companies that depend on Nvidia supply, or that sell chips into sensitive markets, should treat export compliance as an area of heightened scrutiny.

Policy & Regulation Cybersecurity & Infrastructure

Two dozen House Democrats challenge the FAA's nationwide rollout of an AI air traffic tool built under an $875 million contract, Reuters reports

A group of about two dozen House Democrats, led by Representative Rick Larsen, the top Democrat on the House Transportation Committee, has raised concerns about the Federal Aviation Administration's decision to deploy a new AI tool for air traffic controllers across the country, Reuters reports. The lawmakers described the move as the "sudden expansion of a largely untested system" and asked the FAA to answer a set of questions by 30 November.

According to Reuters, the FAA started using the software in the Washington area in September, where it applies predictive analytics to how flights are managed, before deciding to deploy it nationwide on 10 November. The work sits under an $875 million, 12-year contract awarded to Air Space Intelligence. The Reuters report does not include a response from the FAA.

Why it mattersAir traffic control is one of the highest-stakes places where government has put AI into daily operations. Congressional pushback here sets expectations for how much testing evidence agencies, and their contractors, must show before scaling AI in safety-critical systems, a standard that is likely to spread to other infrastructure such as power grids, rail and hospitals.

New York Governor Kathy Hochul signs a five-year ban on toys with built-in AI chatbots, with fines of up to $15,000 a day for each violation

Governor Kathy Hochul signed legislation on Tuesday, 24 November, that pauses for five years the manufacture, distribution and sale in New York of toys that have AI chatbots embedded or built in, the governor's office said. Examples in the announcement include talking stuffed animals, dolls and robots that go online to hold conversations with children. The bill, S.9408A/A.11144B, was sponsored by State Senator Andrew Gounardes and Assemblymember Rebecca Kassay.

The law takes effect 90 days after signing, so it will not apply during this holiday season, according to the release. Attorney General Letitia James will enforce it, with civil penalties of up to $15,000 per day for each violation. The governor's office points to testing by an unnamed national consumer watchdog of chatbot toys marketed to children aged 3 to 12, which found some toys discussing sexually explicit topics, offering unsafe guidance and showing weaker safety limits as conversations ran longer, with few or no parental controls. The measure follows an October New York law that bars certain unsafe chatbot features for minors.

Why it mattersThis is an outright product pause rather than a disclosure rule, and it targets a fast-growing consumer category. Toy makers, AI model providers that license chatbots into devices, and retailers selling into New York now face a hard deadline, and other states watching child safety and AI may copy the approach.

Energy & Utilities Research & Science

AI uses less water per dollar spent than almond milk or golf, an Enverus Intelligence Research study finds, with about 90% of AI's water footprint at power plants, Reuters reports

A new study from Enverus Intelligence Research, reported by Reuters on 24 November, compares the water footprint of AI spending with that of everyday purchases. By the firm's estimate, $25 a month spent on AI accounts for about 1.9 gallons of water, a figure that counts the water used to generate the electricity data centers run on. The same $25 is tied to 308 gallons when spent on almond milk and 679 gallons when spent on golf green fees, while household electricity from a grid running entirely on natural gas uses about 31 gallons for the same outlay.

The report, titled "Data Center Water Use | Setting the Record Straight," concludes that about 90% of the water attributed to running AI models is consumed at power plants rather than at the data centers themselves. It puts a modern hyperscale data center campus at about 32,100 gallons per acre per month, below crops such as rice, almonds or alfalfa and similar to an average US golf course. Ryan Luther, director of Energy Transition Research at Enverus, told Reuters that "skipping one almond latte a week does more than giving up AI chatbots entirely." All figures are Enverus estimates as reported by Reuters.

Why it mattersWater use has become one of the most common local objections to new data centers. If most of AI's water footprint sits at the power plant rather than in the cooling system, the choice of electricity supply matters as much as the building design, and that changes where developers, regulators and corporate buyers should focus. Expect comparisons like these to appear in permitting fights, and expect critics to test the method behind them.
