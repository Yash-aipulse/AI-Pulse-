US stocks are now 10% below their October high as doubts about the AI industry push the market into a correction, AP reports, with history suggesting recoveries take months

The US stock market has dropped 10% from the all-time high it set last month, according to The Associated Press, which is the level Wall Street calls a correction; a fall of at least 20% would count as a bear market. AP ties the slide to the same AI trade that lifted indexes to records in October. Investors worry that AI share prices ran too far, that the strong demand for memory, processors and other data center building blocks could fade if AI proves less profitable than promised, and that the cost of the technology and of data centers keeps rising.

AP lists newer pressures too. The Federal Reserve is expected to raise its short-term interest rate next month, which could make borrowing dearer for AI companies, and investors are uneasy about regulation under a new Congress, where newly elected Democrats are promising to crack down on the industry amid a backlash against data centers. Texas and Georgia, two large states that offer data center incentives, elected Democratic governors who have vowed to stop new construction. AP says the downfall of former Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang has been a big factor in the sell-off, and that Anthropic's market debut this week did not stop it. David Trainer of research firm New Constructs told AP: "The bubble is popping and it has much further to go."

Why it mattersA correction changes the funding climate for AI, not just portfolio values. Higher expected interest rates, falling share prices and political resistance to data centers all raise the cost of capital for the companies building AI capacity, and that can flow through to cloud prices, vendor stability and the pace of new projects. History offers some comfort but no promise: the CFRA figures AP cites show most corrections recover within months, while those that turn into bear markets cut much deeper.

Nvidia has lost more than $2.7 trillion in market value since its 6 October record, nearly $900 billion of it this week, as Trump says Jensen Huang should get a second chance, Yahoo Finance reports

Yahoo Finance reports that Nvidia has shed more than $2.7 trillion in market value since its record high on 6 October, with nearly $900 billion of that lost this week as a reported SEC probe looks into the company's accounting. Jensen Huang, who left the chief executive role barely a week ago, remains Nvidia's largest individual shareholder, with a stake Yahoo values at about $102 billion at Friday's close. At a White House briefing on Friday, President Trump said: "I would love to see Jensen be given a second chance." Elon Musk, asked on X about hiring Huang, replied that it would be an honor.

On the prediction market Polymarket, the most popular bet for Huang's next formal role is a company he starts himself, at 27%, ahead of no new job before 2028 at 24% and a return to Nvidia at 20%; SpaceX gets 8% and Intel 3%. Yahoo stresses the market is small, with about $64,000 traded. Any next step looks some way off: Huang faces a closed-door House interview on 1 December and has agreed to testify publicly before the Senate Banking Committee, and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said on Tuesday that he wants answers from Nvidia's board within 10 days.

Why it mattersThe leadership question at the world's most important AI chip supplier is now being argued in public by the President, the Treasury Secretary and Congress, while the company's value keeps falling. That makes Nvidia's strategy, governance and relationship with Washington less predictable for the customers, partners and investors who depend on it. Until the board's answers and the congressional hearings are on the record, expect continued volatility in Nvidia and in the many AI stocks and budgets tied to it.

Amazon is in advanced talks to buy Decart, a startup whose software helps AI developers switch between chips, in a deal of about $7.5 billion, the Financial Times reports

Amazon has reached advanced discussions to acquire AI startup Decart for about $7.5 billion, according to a Financial Times report on Saturday that cites people familiar with the matter, as summarized by Seeking Alpha. The talks are in their final stages and an agreement could be announced as early as next week, although the report notes the negotiations could still fall apart. This would move forward talks first reported in October by The Wall Street Journal, which then put the deal at nearly $7 billion.

Decart was set up in 2023 by three Israeli engineers, Moshe Shalev and the brothers Dean and Orian Leitersdorf. Its main product is software that lets developers move AI work between different chips, and it was an early adopter of Trainium3, the in-house chip Amazon launched last year to compete with Nvidia's GPUs. The company also develops two world models, Lucy and Oasis, which can edit video live and generate interactive virtual worlds. In May, a $300 million funding round with backers including Sequoia Capital and Nvidia valued Decart at $4 billion.

Why it mattersSoftware that makes AI workloads portable across chips weakens the lock-in that has kept most buyers on Nvidia. If Amazon owns that layer, it can make its own Trainium chips easier to adopt for customers who fear rewriting their code, at a moment when Nvidia faces leadership turmoil and a sliding share price. A purchase at roughly twice Decart's May valuation, in a falling market, also shows the big cloud providers are still willing to pay up for AI infrastructure software that serves their chip strategies.
