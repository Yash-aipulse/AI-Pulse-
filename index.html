NVIDIA announces the Vera Rubin platform in full production at Computex 2026, delivering 10x agent throughput over Blackwell across a supply chain spanning 350+ factories in 30 countries

NVIDIA – the semiconductor company whose GPUs power the majority of AI data centers worldwide – used its Computex 2026 keynote in Taipei on 01 June to announce that the Vera Rubin platform, its next-generation AI factory architecture, has entered full production. The platform unifies five purpose-built rack types into a single POD-scale system: Vera Rubin NVL72 GPU racks, Vera CPU racks, Groq 3 LPX networking, Vera BlueField-4 STX storage, and Spectrum-6 SPX Ethernet racks. NVIDIA claims 10x agent throughput at scale compared with the previous-generation Grace Blackwell platform. The Rubin GPU features two reticle dies delivering 50 PFLOPs of NVFP4 inference and 35 PFLOPs of NVFP4 training performance – 5x and 3.5x increases over Blackwell, respectively – along with HBM4 memory (the latest generation of high-bandwidth memory used in AI accelerators) offering up to 22 TB/s bandwidth per chip, a 2.8x jump over Blackwell.

The production ramp involves hundreds of ecosystem partners – 150 in Taiwan alone – across more than 350 factories in 30 countries. Major system builders in full-scale production include Dell Technologies, HPE, Lenovo, Supermicro, ASUS, Foxconn, GIGABYTE, IBM, Inventec, MSI, and Wistron. NVIDIA also introduced Spectrum-X Ethernet Photonics – now in production – combining co-packaged optics with Spectrum-X switching to enable million-GPU AI factories. Vera Rubin NVL72 racks are 100% liquid cooled with cable-free modular tray designs, reducing installation time from two hours to five minutes compared with Blackwell equivalents. Early Vera CPU customers confirmed by CNBC include Anthropic (the company that makes the Claude AI assistant), OpenAI (the company behind ChatGPT), SpaceX’s xAI, Dell, Oracle, and CoreWeave.

Why it mattersVera Rubin is NVIDIA’s answer to the compute demands of agentic AI workloads – systems where a single prompt can trigger thousands of sequential reasoning, retrieval, and tool-use steps. The 10x throughput improvement and the breadth of the manufacturing ramp signal that NVIDIA is scaling supply ahead of a demand cycle it expects to be driven by AI agents rather than chatbots. The $200 billion total addressable market NVIDIA has cited for Vera CPUs alone could reshape server economics across the industry.

NVIDIA enters the PC processor market with RTX Spark – an Arm-based superchip with 128 GB unified memory targeting Windows laptops from Dell, HP, ASUS, Lenovo, and Microsoft

At the same Computex keynote, NVIDIA CEO Jensen Huang unveiled RTX Spark, NVIDIA’s first-ever processor built specifically for Windows PCs. The Arm-based superchip packs a 20-core Grace CPU (developed alongside MediaTek, the Taiwanese chip design firm), a Blackwell-class RTX GPU with 6,144 CUDA cores, an NPU (neural processing unit – a dedicated chip for running AI tasks efficiently on-device), and up to 128 GB of unified LPDDR5X memory – all on a single TSMC 3 nm package. NVIDIA claims 1 petaFLOP of AI performance from the chip, which it also refers to internally as the N1X.

RTX Spark systems will debut in the fall of 2026, with over 30 laptops and 10 desktops announced from Microsoft (Surface Laptop Ultra), Dell, HP, ASUS, Lenovo, and MSI. NVIDIA says the chip’s GPU is “roughly equivalent” to the RTX 5070 laptop GPU. Alongside RTX Spark, NVIDIA announced DLSS 4.5 Ray Reconstruction, expected in August 2026, and confirmed it is working with all major game developers to bring popular anti-cheat software natively to Windows on Arm. The move marks NVIDIA’s first entry into the PC processor market in over a decade and represents a direct challenge to Apple’s M-series silicon, Qualcomm’s Snapdragon X platform, and the long-standing Intel/AMD x86 duopoly in Windows PCs.

Why it mattersNVIDIA is leveraging its AI and GPU dominance to attempt what no chip designer has managed: breaking Apple’s grip on premium Arm-based personal computing and Qualcomm’s early lead in Windows on Arm. The 128 GB unified memory configuration – matching Apple’s highest-end MacBook Pro – positions RTX Spark for on-device AI workloads that most current Windows laptops cannot handle. If NVIDIA’s ecosystem play succeeds, it could reshape the PC supply chain, divert OEM roadmaps, and create a third axis of competition in a market Intel and AMD have owned for decades.

GitHub Copilot switches to token-based billing on 01 June, ending flat-rate pricing – developers report projected cost increases of 10x to 50x

GitHub Copilot’s new usage-based billing system took effect on 01 June 2026, replacing the flat subscription model with token-based “GitHub AI Credits.” Under the new model, monthly subscription prices remain nominally unchanged – Pro at $10/month, Pro+ at $39/month, Business at $19/user/month, Enterprise at $39/user/month – but each plan now purchases a fixed allotment of AI Credits (1 credit = $0.01) that are consumed based on actual token usage across inputs, outputs, and cached context. Code completions and Next Edit suggestions remain included at no credit cost, but chat, agentic coding (where the AI takes multi-step actions autonomously), and code review sessions now consume credits at model API rates.

The change has generated significant developer backlash. TechCrunch reported that developers have flooded Reddit and X with complaints, with some reporting projected cost increases from $29/month to $750/month and from $50/month to $3,000/month. The previous fallback mechanism – which switched users to a lower-cost model when premium requests were exhausted – has been eliminated. GitHub is offering promotional credits for June, July, and August to ease the transition and has introduced pooled usage for organizations. Annual plans are being retired; annual subscribers will remain on existing terms until expiration. GitHub CPO Mario Rodriguez wrote that the change was necessary because Copilot now powers “far more complex, agentic workflows that consume far more compute.” The timing is notable: it coincides with reports that Claude Code – Anthropic’s command-line coding tool – has overtaken Copilot as the dominant developer AI tool, per The Information.

Why it mattersThis is the first major AI developer tool to abandon unlimited-use pricing in favor of metered economics, and it exposes the unsustainable cost structure behind compute-intensive AI coding workflows. Microsoft’s simultaneous announcement of homegrown MAI coding models for Build 2026 (Story 5) suggests the company is hedging against Copilot revenue compression by building an in-house alternative. For the broader AI tools market, this signals that the subscription-to-metered transition may become the norm for compute-intensive AI products.

Microsoft clarifies it will not pursue legal action against security researchers after the Nightmare Eclipse zero-day controversy – patches for six exploited vulnerabilities remain pending until 09 June

On 01 June 2026, Microsoft’s Security Response Center (MSRC) issued a public statement on X clarifying that it has “no intention to pursue action against individuals conducting or publishing their security research.” The statement was a direct response to widespread backlash triggered by Microsoft’s 28 May MSRC blog post, which condemned an anonymous researcher known as Nightmare Eclipse for releasing six unpatched Windows zero-day exploits – previously unknown vulnerabilities with no available fix – between April and mid-May 2026. The blog post was widely interpreted as a blanket legal threat against the security research community.

Nightmare Eclipse released working proof-of-concept exploit code for six vulnerabilities targeting Windows Defender (Microsoft’s built-in antivirus software) and BitLocker (Microsoft’s drive encryption tool): BlueHammer (CVE-2026-33825), RedSun (CVE-2026-41091), UnDefend (CVE-2026-45498), YellowKey (CVE-2026-45585), GreenPlasma, and MiniPlasma. At least three of these – BlueHammer, RedSun, and UnDefend – have been confirmed as actively exploited in real-world attacks, a fact corroborated by CISA’s (the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency) addition to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog. Microsoft’s patches are not expected until the 09 June Patch Tuesday cycle; no out-of-band release has been committed. Nightmare Eclipse, who moved to GitLab after GitHub removed their account on 23 May, has threatened additional disclosures for 14 July.

Why it mattersThis controversy exposes a structural vulnerability in the coordinated disclosure model that underpins enterprise cybersecurity. When a researcher perceives that the vendor has acted in bad faith – in this case, allegedly revoking portal access and withholding bug bounty compensation – the incentive to cooperate collapses. The 18-day gap between the first public exploit and the expected patch date leaves every Windows enterprise exposed. More broadly, the incident demonstrates that productivity gains in exploit development have outpaced vendor patch velocity – six zero-days in six weeks is a cadence most security teams are not staffed to handle.

Microsoft prepares to unveil homegrown MAI coding and multimodal AI models at Build 2026 on 02 June, signaling a move to reduce its dependency on OpenAI

Microsoft is preparing to unveil a suite of in-house AI models at its Build developer conference opening 02 June in San Francisco, including a coding model designed to power GitHub Copilot and compete directly with Anthropic’s Claude Code, per reporting from The Information and Reuters. The models will target Azure AI Foundry, Copilot, Teams, and Windows integration. The coding model specifically is aimed at reclaiming GitHub Copilot’s competitive position after Claude Code reportedly overtook it as the dominant developer AI tool. Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella’s opening keynote on 02 June at 12:30 PM ET is expected to frame Windows as a platform for AI agents. The MAI model family was formed ~six months ago under Mustafa Suleyman’s AI division and has already released transcription, audio generation, and image generation models. Build 2026 marks the most significant public debut of the in-house stack.

Why it mattersFor three years, Microsoft’s AI strategy has relied on OpenAI’s models as the core intelligence layer across its products. The simultaneous launch of a coding model, image model, voice model, and transcription model signals a deliberate diversification away from single-vendor dependency. If Microsoft’s coding model proves competitive with Claude Code and GPT-based Copilot, it could reduce the company’s OpenAI compute costs and give it full control of the model-to-product pipeline – a strategic imperative ahead of OpenAI’s own Q4 2026 IPO.

SpaceX makes its S-1 public ahead of a record-breaking IPO at a $1.75 trillion target valuation – the filing reveals xAI lost $6.4 billion in 2025 as the roadshow targets 04–11 June

SpaceX plans to earmark a large share for retail investors and will host 1,500 retail investors at a dedicated event, with participants from the US, UK, EU, Australia, Canada, Japan, and South Korea. Morgan Stanley, Bank of America, Citigroup, JP Morgan, and Goldman Sachs are leading the deal.

Why it mattersThe SpaceX IPO is the most significant public market event for AI in 2026, because it forces public investors to price xAI’s losses against Starlink’s profitability within a single vehicle. The $6.4 billion xAI loss in 2025 reveals the true cost of frontier model training at scale – a figure that investors in other AI companies can use as a benchmark. The $1.75 trillion target valuation implies that investors are pricing $1 trillion or more of future value from currently unprofitable businesses. The IPO’s success or failure will set the tone for OpenAI’s and Anthropic’s own planned listings later in 2026.
