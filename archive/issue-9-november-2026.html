Nvidia's board opens an independent investigation into CEO Jensen Huang after reports he was the target of a Chinese intelligence effort, Al Jazeera and Reuters report

Nvidia said on Sunday, 8 November 2026, that a committee of independent directors, working with outside counsel, will investigate chief executive Jensen Huang, according to Al Jazeera, reporting with Reuters. The board said it has no reason to believe that US national security or Nvidia's secrets were compromised, that it remains confident in Huang and his leadership team, and that Huang will stay on as CEO while the review runs. In an email to staff the same day, Huang admitted to an extramarital relationship and apologized to his family.

The probe follows New York Times reporting last month that Huang may have been the target of a Chinese espionage plot, and a 29 October Times report that a Chinese woman, Alice Huang, also known as Huang Le, tried to build close ties with him to learn about Nvidia's technology. She is not related to Jensen Huang. Her employer, Megaspeed, grew into one of Nvidia's largest buyers, purchasing about $4.8bn in equipment, according to the Times. The Chinese Embassy in Washington has denied the spying claims. In Congress, Democratic Senator Elizabeth Warren said Huang and the board "owe Congress answers," while Republican lawmakers John Moolenaar, Brian Mast and Rick Crawford asked Huang to testify on 18 November.

Why it mattersNvidia supplies most of the chips behind the AI buildout, and its sales to China are already a political flashpoint. A governance review of its CEO, combined with a congressional push for testimony, adds leadership and policy risk to a company that buyers, investors and governments treat as critical infrastructure, and it is likely to draw more scrutiny to who ultimately ends up with advanced AI chips.

Citigroup cuts US equities to neutral from overweight over AI-driven volatility and moves global health care to overweight, Reuters reports

Citigroup's strategists have lowered their view on US stocks to "neutral" from "overweight," Reuters reported on 9 November 2026, pointing to the volatility around AI shares. The bank also took global and European technology sectors down to neutral from overweight. On the other side of the ledger, Citi raised its stance on European equities outside the UK, which it says carry less exposure to tech swings and benefit more from falling oil prices, and lifted both global health care and European food and beverages to overweight from neutral.

According to Reuters, the call follows a selloff fueled by concern over heavy AI spending and tighter rules, which wiped $3 trillion off the market value of S&P 500 companies. Democratic gains in last week's US midterms, including in states where resistance to new data centers has grown, added to fears that regulation could slow the AI infrastructure buildout. In a note on Monday, strategists led by Beata Manthey said they worry positioning on indices such as the Nasdaq could "reset lower into net-short territory." Citi added that the volatility has spread across regions and sectors over the past few days.

Why it mattersWhen a major bank's strategists step back from US stocks and tech specifically because of AI, it signals that the AI trade is now being treated as a source of risk rather than a reason to stay overweight. That shift can raise the cost of capital for AI-exposed companies and push investors toward defensive sectors such as health care.

Banks and credit investors grow warier of AI debt after the selloff, with some reluctant to fund deals even when backed by Anthropic and Google, Reuters reports

Lenders and credit investors are looking harder at AI-related debt and are hesitant to take on more exposure to some borrowers, Reuters reported from New York on 9 November 2026, citing unnamed sources. Two of the sources said the change has happened only in the past few days. Some lenders are reluctant to finance deals even when they are backed by Anthropic and Google, according to the sources, who did not name the lenders; Anthropic and Google declined to comment. Reuters says the pullback was worsened by Thursday's market drop, which erased $2.5 trillion from the Nasdaq 100 and pushed it into a correction, and by midterm results that raised the prospect of more oversight of data centers.

The amounts at stake are large. Goldman Sachs Research expects global hyperscaler debt issuance to reach $420 billion in 2027, more than 60% above its $250 billion estimate for fiscal 2026. At JPMorgan Chase, loans backed by income-producing properties, a category that includes data centers, totaled $29 billion at the end of the third quarter, its second-largest commercial real estate exposure at 12%, up from 8% three years earlier. Bank of America analyst Yuri Seliger said spreads on 10-year hyperscaler bonds have widened by about 50 basis points this year, against roughly 20 for high-quality industrial companies not tied to AI. CoreWeave's stock is down 35% this year, and neocloud firm Nebius, which decided against a high-yield bond it had explored in September, took a $3.5 billion senior secured loan facility from Citigroup in October. "Most banks and credit investors will take a wait and see approach," said Jay Bala, CEO of asset manager AIP.

Why it mattersThe AI buildout increasingly runs on borrowed money, not just cash flow. If banks and bond investors become more selective, the cost and availability of debt will start to decide which data centers and neocloud capacity actually get built, and on what timeline, which matters to everyone buying AI compute on multi-year contracts.

Apple CEO John Ternus is pressing software chief Craig Federighi to speed up Apple's AI plans and go beyond fixing Siri, Bloomberg's Mark Gurman reports

Apple chief executive John Ternus has privately urged Craig Federighi, Apple's senior vice president of software engineering, to move faster on artificial intelligence and to aim higher than the Siri repairs the company has been working through, according to Bloomberg's Mark Gurman in his Sunday Power On newsletter, as summarized by Gizmodo on 8 November 2026. Gurman's sources describe Ternus as frustrated that Apple has not treated AI with the same urgency as its competitors, and say the push came in a closed-door meeting. Federighi has been seen as skeptical of AI, with concerns that include privacy, security and the effect on jobs.

The report also says Mike Rockwell, Apple's vice president of Siri engineering, is weighing whether to step back from that role or leave Apple next year. If he does, his Siri AI work could pass to Amar Subramanya, a Google veteran Apple hired last year, and Sebastien Marineau, one of Federighi's deputies. The Siri upgrade in iOS 27 delivered much of what Apple promised in 2024 but still cannot run long agentic tasks in the background, and Gurman reports that Apple wants Siri to become more agentic; Ternus called agentic AI "promising" on a recent earnings call. For iOS 28 and its other next major operating system releases, Apple is reportedly giving teams more time for coding and exploring features and less time for folding changes into the software.

Why it mattersA push from the top to move faster suggests Apple may widen its AI ambitions beyond Siri fixes in its next software cycle, which would change what enterprise IT teams, app developers and Apple's AI partners need to plan for. Possible leadership changes in the Siri group add execution risk, and the reported shift in how Apple builds iOS 28 points to bigger AI changes arriving with next year's software rather than right away.

Tencent's chief AI scientist warns AI models will soon pose biology risks, days after Tencent launched its Hy4 model, the South China Morning Post reports

Yao Shunyu, Tencent's chief AI scientist, told a science event in Hong Kong on Saturday that AI models are nearing the point where their biology abilities could be misused, as their cyber abilities already can, according to the South China Morning Post, in a report summarized by The Next Web on 8 November 2026. Models will "reach a threshold where it's actually possible to do harm," he said. Yao argued that Chinese companies releasing open models should take safety very seriously, and that biology risks may be harder to manage than cyber risks.

His comments came days after Tencent launched its flagship Hy4 model on 4 November. Tencent says it left material on exploiting software flaws out of Hy4's training data and used defensive security data instead, and that, based on internal expert reviews, the model has strong basic biology knowledge but is weaker than leading models at designing experiments. Tencent plans to release Hy4's weights and a safety testing report in the coming days, Reuters reported, and benchmarking firm Artificial Analysis ranks Hy4 as the second-best Chinese model, according to the SCMP. Yao said US companies such as Anthropic make a strong case for keeping top models closed, but argued that a mix of open and closed models may work best because open models can also help defenders.

Why it mattersA senior leader at a major Chinese lab is publicly naming biology as the next misuse risk while his company prepares to publish an open-weight model with a safety report attached. For companies weighing open models, safety evidence is becoming part of the product, but once weights are released the developer has little control over how they are used, so buyers need to judge that evidence themselves.

Trump tells 60 Minutes that AI risks can be managed and says he is open to guardrails, but not to rules that leave the US behind China, CBS News reports

In a post-midterm interview with CBS's 60 Minutes, published on 8 November 2026, President Donald Trump told interviewer Ross Douthat that he is bullish on AI and that its dangers are smaller than some in the industry claim and can be managed. When Douthat raised warnings from AI executives of a 10% chance that the technology could kill humanity, Trump replied that he did not see the risk as being nearly as real as they describe.

Asked whether he would work with Democrats on AI safety, Trump said he would, adding "I don't mind guardrails," but he said he did not want rules that would leave the US far behind China. He described AI as a race with China that the winner takes, argued that China has no guardrails of its own and will build the technology regardless, and said Chinese President Xi Jinping is fully committed to what Trump calls superintelligence. In the exchange, Trump did not say which specific safeguards he would support.

Why it mattersThis is the clearest signal since the midterms that the White House may be willing to negotiate on AI safeguards rather than reject them outright, but competitiveness against China is the test any rule will have to pass. Companies should plan for the possibility of targeted federal guardrails while assuming that measures seen as slowing US development will face resistance.

Nvidia signs an agreement with SK Telecom and Korean chip designer Rebellions to run Rebellions' AI chips alongside Nvidia GPUs in the same data center, aiming for commercial use in 2028

Nvidia, SK Telecom and Korean AI chip startup Rebellions signed a memorandum of understanding at Nvidia's AI Day Seoul 2026 on 9 November, the Seoul Economic Daily reports. The plan is a combined system in which Nvidia GPUs and Rebellions' neural processing units (NPUs) split AI inference work inside one data center. Rebellions' next-generation R200 chips would be linked through NVLink Fusion, the Nvidia interconnect first shown at Computex in May 2025, and Nvidia is opening its MGX server rack design to chips from outside companies so the setup fits its ecosystem. SK Telecom will help test the system on its own infrastructure, and the companies are targeting commercial deployment in 2028.

Rebellions, which merged with SK Telecom's former chip affiliate Sapeon Korea in December 2024, has not disclosed the R200's specifications. Its current R100 chip uses four HBM3E memory stacks with 144 GB of capacity and 4.8 TB/s of bandwidth, and the paper says, citing unnamed sources, that Samsung Electronics supplies that memory. Rebellions CEO Park Sung-hyun said at the signing that the company ultimately wants "to become an exporter of tokens," referring to the units of AI output. A senior official from Korea's Ministry of Science and ICT attended the signing.

Why it mattersNvidia is starting to let rival inference chips sit inside its own rack and interconnect design rather than compete only from outside it. If this works, data center operators could mix lower-cost specialist chips with Nvidia GPUs in one system, which would change how AI capacity is bought, priced and sourced, and gives countries such as Korea a route into Nvidia-based infrastructure for their own chipmakers.

AI data centers turn to used gas turbines and converted jet engines as new US turbine deliveries now take about six years on average, Reuters reports

Developers racing to power AI data centers are buying second-hand gas turbines, refurbishing idle units and adapting retired jet engines because new equipment is booked years ahead, according to a Reuters analysis published on 9 November 2026. Wood Mackenzie told Reuters that worldwide gas turbine orders exceed 100 GW against estimated global production of 60 GW to 70 GW, that average US delivery times have reached six years, and that US delivery talks now center on 2030 and 2031. Jefferies analysts estimate US data centers account for nearly 17 GW of demand this year and say that could more than triple by 2028.

The price gap is wide. Aurora Energy Research puts new turbines at about $2 million to $2.5 million per MW. Reuters cites a recent sale of two 14.5-MW Solar Turbines units, made by a Caterpillar business, for $12.5 million, or about $430,000 per MW, according to Dan Poole of broker ReflowX; new equivalents would cost about $35 million and take five years. Crusoe ordered 13 aeroderivative turbines based on GE Aerospace jet engines from ProEnergy in April, with 650 MW of combined capacity for its US AI data center projects, and Wärtsilä has booked around 3 GW of gas-engine orders from US data centers since spring last year. Enverus analyst Noor Qureshi told Reuters the used market is unlikely to cover much of the shortfall, and that air permits and grid access also cause delays.

Why it mattersPower, not chips, is becoming the pacing item for new AI capacity. When turbine orders run well ahead of factory output, the timing and cost of data center projects depend on who can secure generating equipment, permits and grid connections, and buyers of AI compute will feel that in delivery dates and prices.
