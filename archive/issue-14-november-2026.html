Nvidia's board has approached Arm CEO Rene Haas about succeeding Jensen Huang, and Arm shares fall more than 5%, Reuters reports, citing the Financial Times

Nvidia's hunt for a permanent chief executive has reached one of the chip industry's best-known leaders. On Friday, 13 November 2026, the Financial Times reported, citing people familiar with the matter, that Nvidia's board has approached Arm Holdings CEO Rene Haas as a possible successor to Jensen Huang, Reuters reports. According to the FT, Haas is one of several candidates and there is "no guarantee he will be offered the job or accept it." Reuters said it could not independently verify the report. Huang stepped down on Thursday, and finance chief Colette Kress is serving as interim CEO.

Haas is no stranger to Nvidia. Reuters notes that he spent seven years there, rising to vice president and general manager of its computing products business, before moving to Arm in 2013. He became Arm's chief executive in 2022 and led the company's Nasdaq listing in 2023. At the time of the Reuters report, Arm's US-listed shares had pared some losses after the news but were still down more than 5%, and Nvidia's shares were down more than 3%. Arm declined to comment, and Nvidia did not immediately respond to a request from Reuters. Later on Friday, Nvidia's board pushed back, saying it had not formally engaged any potential candidates to succeed Huang, The Motley Fool reports; by Friday afternoon Arm's shares were down 4.96%, at roughly $189.

Why it mattersWhoever runs Nvidia next will shape the pricing, product roadmap and export-policy stance of the main supplier of AI accelerators. The fall in Arm's shares on a single press report shows that the succession search is now creating uncertainty at a second major chip company, not just at Nvidia, and that investors expect a long and visible process rather than a quick appointment.

Financial Services Hardware & Devices

Chip stocks slide again on Friday, with the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index down 6% and AMD down 10% by late morning, Reuters reports

The sell-off in AI hardware shares spread well beyond Nvidia on Friday, 13 November 2026. Reuters reported that, as of 11:23 a.m. Eastern time, the Nasdaq Composite was down 1.74% and the S&P 500 down 1.01%, while the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index had fallen 6%. AMD was down 10%, Broadcom 7% and Nvidia 4.3%, and GPU cloud providers CoreWeave and Nebius were each 10% lower. Technology was the weakest sector in the S&P 500, down 3%.

Not every AI name fell. Reuters said Cerebras rose more than 6%, and Apple and Meta were the only gainers among the Magnificent Seven, each up less than 1%. Investors were also weighing October producer prices, which rose 6.5% from a year earlier, the largest annual increase since November 2022. These are intraday figures from late morning in New York; closing levels may differ.

Why it mattersThe selling now reaches the rivals and cloud providers that were expected to benefit from Nvidia's troubles, which suggests investors are repricing AI infrastructure as a whole rather than one company. A weaker market for these shares can raise the cost of capital for neoclouds and other buyers of AI hardware that fund expansion through equity and convertible debt.

Financial Services Operations & Enterprise

OpenAI launches ChatGPT Wallet, letting its AI agents complete purchases with stored cards for a limited group of US Pro subscribers, TechCrunch reports

OpenAI introduced ChatGPT Wallet on Friday, 13 November 2026, TechCrunch reports. The feature lets ChatGPT's agents, which OpenAI calls dots, go beyond suggesting what to buy and complete the checkout for the user. A user adds eligible payment cards once; when a purchase is made, the Wallet charges the chosen card through a payment token, so neither the agent nor the merchant sees the real card details. The merchant receives the shipping address and contact information it needs, and purchases keep their card rewards and protections such as dispute rights.

At launch, access is limited to a small number of US ChatGPT Pro subscribers aged 18 or older who hold a US-issued Visa, Mastercard or American Express card. Before each checkout the user reviews the items, the total and the card, and then approves with a security check such as Face ID or a fingerprint, which creates a record of the transaction. OpenAI plans to add spending limits and rules by purchase category or time period so users can pre-approve some purchases, and it has published a Commerce Partner Playbook for retailers that want to support agent-led shopping. TechCrunch reported no pricing and no date for broader availability.

Why it mattersThis moves AI agents from recommending products to actually spending money, with tokenized cards and biometric approval as the safeguards. Retailers, card issuers and employers will need clear rules for purchases that an agent starts on a customer's or employee's behalf, including how disputes and fraud checks work when no human typed in the card.

Venture Capital Operations & Enterprise

LangChain raises $300 million at a $3 billion valuation in a round led by CapitalG, and says more than 7,000 organizations use its LangSmith agent platform

San Francisco-based LangChain, which sells the LangSmith platform for running and managing AI agents, said on 13 November 2026 that it has raised $300 million at a $3 billion valuation. Existing investor CapitalG led the round. New backers include GIC, DFJ Growth, Salesforce Ventures, Insight Venture Partners, QuantumLight and MongoDB Ventures, and existing investors IVP, Sapphire Ventures, Sequoia, Amplify and Benchmark also took part. LangChain says it has now raised $460 million in total.

According to the company, more than 7,000 organizations use LangSmith, which is organized around three areas: running agents, improving them and governing them. The announcement cites Workday cutting the time to deploy a new agent from five months to under a day, and Clay monitoring hundreds of millions of agent runs a month. Chief executive Harrison Chase argued that companies should be able to choose the best model for each task rather than being tied to one provider. LangChain plans to show new LangSmith features at an event on 20 November 2026.

Why it mattersInvestors are paying up for the software layer that sits between companies and the model providers, where agents are deployed, monitored and controlled. For enterprises, the pitch is independence from any single model vendor, which matters as model prices, terms and the providers themselves keep changing.

Pharma & Health Operations & Enterprise

Wolters Kluwer agrees to buy clinical AI company AgileMD for up to $100 million, including $40 million upfront, to bring UpToDate into hospital workflows

Wolters Kluwer Health said on 13 November 2026 that it will acquire AgileMD, a San Francisco company founded in 2011, for up to $100 million. It will pay $40 million upfront, with the remainder depending on AgileMD meeting performance targets. AgileMD has two products: Pathways, which builds a hospital's own clinical guidelines into the electronic health record, and eCART, an FDA-cleared AI engine that monitors patients in real time. AgileMD expects annual recurring revenue of $7 million by the end of 2026, and all 23 of its employees will join Wolters Kluwer.

Wolters Kluwer plans to combine AgileMD with UpToDate Expert AI, its clinical AI tool, which it says has been adopted by more than 2,700 US hospitals and health systems since its launch in September 2025. The goal is to put UpToDate's guidance directly into clinicians' daily workflow and digital health applications. The company expects the deal to close in the first quarter of 2027 or sooner, subject to customary conditions and government approvals, to have an immaterial effect on adjusted earnings, and to return at least its 8% after-tax cost of capital within three to five years.

Why it mattersClinical reference content is moving from a separate lookup tool into the record where doctors place orders, with AI monitoring alongside it. At up to about 14.3 times AgileMD's expected year-end recurring revenue (calculated: $100 million divided by $7 million), the price shows what large information providers will pay for workflow-embedded clinical AI.
