Oracle builds OpenAI's ChatGPT and Codex into its business software, so employees can run approved Oracle workflows from ChatGPT and build apps without writing code

At its AI World event in Las Vegas on Tuesday, Oracle announced a group of products built with OpenAI. The main one, Oracle AI Workspace, places OpenAI's frontier models alongside Oracle's applications, data and cloud services in a single interface. Through plugins in ChatGPT, staff can look up information, carry out approved business workflows and take actions inside Oracle systems. Oracle says the workspace links to Oracle Fusion Applications, Oracle NetSuite, Oracle AI Database and Oracle Cloud Infrastructure (OCI), as well as other enterprise tools and data, and that governance, approvals and controls stay in place.

Three further offerings were announced alongside it. Oracle AI Security Mesh is a managed service that uses OpenAI cyber models to find and fix weaknesses in applications and infrastructure on OCI, on other clouds and on premises, with customers deciding where testing is authorized and how far it goes. Oracle App Composer lets employees without coding skills build business applications with Codex, while Oracle runs deployment and operations on OCI under enterprise identity, permission, network and audit controls. For smaller companies, a free-to-start Oracle AI SMB Bundle packages ChatGPT Business, AI Workspace, Codex, App Composer, OCI and NetSuite QuickStart, and includes $12,000 of Oracle cloud credits a year, subject to terms and restrictions. Oracle gave no release dates and says timing and pricing may change. OpenAI president Greg Brockman said the wider partnership "helps bring that opportunity to more companies."

Why it mattersOracle runs finance, HR, supply chain and procurement systems for many large companies. Letting ChatGPT act as a front door to those systems moves AI from a side tool into the core back office, where actions have financial and audit consequences. The promise is less time spent navigating screens; the test will be whether approvals, permissions and audit trails hold up when an assistant, rather than a person, starts a transaction. The no-code App Composer also means business teams could build their own applications on company data, which IT will need to govern.

Oracle widens the AI chips on its cloud, adding Google's TPUs and inference chips from Cerebras and SambaNova as it grows Nvidia Vera Rubin capacity to multi-gigawatt scale

Oracle will offer Google's tensor processing units (TPUs) on its cloud for the first time, Reuters reported on Tuesday, and also plans to offer chips from Cerebras Systems and Intel-backed SambaNova Systems, both of which design chips for inference, the stage where AI systems answer user requests. At the same time Oracle is expanding its Nvidia deals: the two companies say Oracle's cloud will grow to multi-gigawatt scale using Nvidia's newest Vera Rubin servers. They did not disclose the value of the deal. Reuters adds that some Nvidia-based systems will use Oracle's in-house Acceleron networking chips instead of certain Nvidia networking chips.

Reuters notes that Nvidia dominates chips for training AI models, while the market for inference is more competitive and varied. "Different AI workloads have different compute needs," said Mahesh Thiagarajan, president of Oracle Cloud Infrastructure, according to Reuters. Oracle did not say how it will pay for the chips. On its most recent earnings call it said customers are increasingly paying for chips, and it kept its capital spending target of about $90 billion to $95 billion for fiscal 2027. For scale, Reuters recalls that Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang has said Vera Rubin systems can generate up to $40 billion of revenue per gigawatt for Nvidia. Oracle plans to hold an investor day this week.

Why it mattersA large cloud provider offering Google's own AI chips, plus two specialist inference chipmakers, next to Nvidia's flagship systems shows that buyers want a choice of hardware matched to each job, especially for the fast-growing work of running models rather than training them. For enterprises, more chip options in one cloud can mean better prices and availability for inference. For investors, the open question is financing: Oracle is committing to multi-gigawatt capacity without saying how it will fund the purchases.

Google DeepMind previews Genie 4, a world model that generates interactive scenes with sound and lets users change them while the simulation runs

Google DeepMind on Tuesday released Genie 4, a model that generates interactive, simulated worlds in real time, as a research preview for trusted testers, TechCrunch reports. Over the coming weeks it will reach Google AI Ultra subscribers through Project Genie, and early access partners of the Gemini API and Google AI Studio. New in this version is sound generated in real time, including music, background noise and dialogue that responds to the user.

Users can also make free-form changes while a simulation is running, such as switching the weather or the camera angle, or adding people and objects to the scene. According to TechCrunch, Genie 4 can keep a scene consistent for up to two minutes, twice as long as its predecessor, Genie 3, which launched as a research preview a little more than a year ago. The report gives no figures for pricing or resolution.

Why it mattersWorld models like Genie are an early step toward software that can simulate an environment instead of just describing it. For businesses, the near-term uses are in prototyping games, ads and media concepts, and in creating varied practice environments for robots and AI agents. Two minutes of consistent scene is still short and the model is only in preview, so this is a technology to watch and test, not one to build production plans on yet.

China's CXMT plans to start making HBM3E memory for AI chips within about two months, even though only 20% to 30% of the chips are usable, Reuters reports

ChangXin Memory Technologies (CXMT) intends to begin producing HBM3E, the high-bandwidth memory that sits beside AI processors, despite weak manufacturing yields, according to an exclusive Reuters report on Wednesday that cites two people familiar with the matter. The sources put CXMT's yields at about 20% to 30%, compared with around 70% at SK hynix. CXMT has sent samples to several Chinese AI chip designers and expects limited production to begin within about two months. The Information first reported the plans; CXMT did not respond to Reuters' request for comment.

The sources also told Reuters that, in testing, CXMT's HBM3E performed closer to SK hynix's older HBM3 than to its HBM3E, and one said the Chinese part is expected to cost more than comparable products from overseas. Reuters links the push to shortages: the US tightened export controls on certain advanced HBM bound for China in December 2024, and Chinese chip designers including Huawei, Cambricon, MetaX and Iluvatar CoreX have raised AI chip prices by 20% to 50% as memory costs climbed. Citing Counterpoint Research, Reuters says SK hynix held 50% of HBM revenue in the second quarter, Samsung 33% and Micron 18%.

Why it mattersHigh-bandwidth memory is now one of the tightest links in the AI supply chain, and China's attempt to make its own shows how export controls are pushing it to build a separate supply, even at low yields and higher cost. For buyers outside China, the report is a reminder that the three established suppliers still control the market, so memory prices and lead times will keep shaping the cost of AI hardware. For anyone tracking China's AI chips, memory supply is a practical limit on how many accelerators its designers can ship.

Chip stocks lead Wall Street higher for a third straight session as Anthropic's IPO details revive the AI trade, while Meta slips after lifting its equity raise to $97.5 billion

US stocks closed higher on Tuesday in a rally led by technology shares, and investor interest in the details of Anthropic's upcoming initial public offering lifted chip stocks for a third session in a row, according to Reuters. The Nasdaq Composite gained 1.18% to 26,477.81 and the S&P 500 added 0.60% to 7,722.60, while the Dow Jones Industrial Average finished almost flat, up 0.03%. Chuck Carlson, chief executive of Horizon Investment Services, told Reuters that chip stocks had been hit hard in recent weeks and were "probably due for a bounce."

The rebound comes after a difficult month for the AI trade. Reuters says worries about whether debt-funded AI spending can last, as borrowing costs rise, have weighed on these stocks in October. The Philadelphia SE Semiconductor index is still more than 20% below its record close of 22 June, although it is up 62.1% so far this year, compared with 12.8% for the S&P 500. Meta shares fell 1.6% after the company increased what Reuters calls its largest-ever equity fundraising, which will pay for its AI expansion, to $97.5 billion. Reuters also pointed to economic data showing rising new orders for computers and peripheral equipment as support for the view that AI-related spending remains strong.

Why it mattersThe AI trade now moves on financing news. One company's IPO filing can lift chip stocks for days, while a very large share sale can weigh on the company raising the money. For businesses that buy AI capacity, these swings matter because many suppliers fund their build-outs through equity and debt markets, and the semiconductor index is still well below its June peak. The rebound so far is a partial recovery, not a return to earlier highs.

Baker Hughes wins a 1.1 GW on-site power order from a major US technology company for AI computing, as its third-quarter orders rise 31% to $10.8 billion

Energy technology group Baker Hughes reported third-quarter 2026 results after the US market close on Tuesday and named two large power orders tied to AI. The bigger one is an integrated order to supply 1.1 gigawatts (GW) of power to a major US technology company it did not name. Baker Hughes says the order covers 43 NovaLT and Frame 5 gas turbine packages, along with gearboxes and BRUSH generators, for computing infrastructure that supports AI model training, inference and physical AI. A second order, for about 500 megawatts (MW) of capacity, will supply 18 Frame 5 gas turbine generator packages with BRUSH generators to a US energy company's data center projects.

Total orders for the quarter were $10,764 million, up 31% from $8,207 million a year earlier, and revenue rose 14% to $8,025 million. Orders in its IET segment rose 42% to $5,886 million, and the company says it is raising its full-year IET order guidance. Its remaining performance obligations, the value of work already contracted, stood at $46.4 billion, including a record $39.4 billion for IET. The chief executive pointed to "broad-based demand across data centers, space, LNG and gas infrastructure."

Why it mattersWhere a grid connection would take years, AI data center builders are often turning to their own gas-fired generation. A single technology company buying 1.1 GW of on-site turbines shows how big these private power plants have become, and the record IET backlog suggests turbine and generator capacity is being booked well ahead. For anyone planning new AI capacity, power equipment lead times and fuel supply are now as important as chip supply, and on-site gas generation brings its own permitting and emissions questions.

SK hynix posts a record 83.3 trillion won quarterly operating profit as AI memory sales lift revenue above 100 trillion won for the first time

South Korean memory maker SK hynix released preliminary third-quarter 2026 results early on Wednesday, Korea time. Revenue reached 104,615.5 billion won, up 32% on the previous quarter and 328% on a year earlier, the first time a quarter has passed 100 trillion won. Operating profit came to 83,333.8 billion won, up 632% year on year, an operating margin of 80%. Net income was 39,775.6 billion won, down 58% from the second quarter. The company credits AI servers: shipments of its newest high-bandwidth memory, HBM4, ramped up during the quarter, and sales of server DDR5 grew.

SK hynix expects HBM4 to overtake the older HBM3E in both shipments and revenue in the fourth quarter, and says it sees demand running ahead of supply through 2030. It has signed long-term agreements with 14 major customers, including global cloud providers, which cover more than half of its production capacity for 2027 and 2028 and typically run five years. It plans a significant rise in investment next year as the first phase of its Yongin site enters full operation, without giving a figure. The board approved a third-quarter dividend of 14 trillion won (20,000 won a share, half of it a special payout from gains on its Kioxia stake) and a 20 trillion won share buyback, and the company targets about 100 trillion won in shareholder returns for 2026.

Why it mattersMemory has become one of the tightest links in the AI supply chain, and SK hynix's results show how much pricing power that gives its makers. Long-term contracts already covering more than half of its 2027 and 2028 capacity mean much of the next two years' supply is spoken for. Companies building or renting AI servers should expect memory to stay a constraint on cost and delivery, and investors now have a clear read on how much AI spending is flowing to suppliers beyond the chip designers.

Guidewire agrees to buy AI underwriting software maker hyperexponential for $1.7 billion in cash, betting on AI agents that help insurers price risk

Insurance software company Guidewire said on Tuesday it has signed a definitive agreement to acquire London-based hyperexponential, which sells AI-native underwriting and pricing software to commercial property and casualty insurers. Guidewire will pay $1.7 billion in cash, subject to adjustments, plus up to $400 million more if specified recurring revenue targets are met. It will fund the deal with $1.0 billion of new term loans, committed by Bank of America and Citibank, and cash on hand. The deal is expected to close in the second quarter of Guidewire's fiscal 2027.

Guidewire says the combination will let it lead in what it calls "agentic underwriting," where AI agents work alongside underwriters and actuaries to assess, price and decide on risks. Hyperexponential's software has supported underwriting decisions on more than $75 billion of annual commercial premium, and its named customers include Aviva, Allianz, Sompo, Beazley and Markel. Guidewire expects the purchase to add at least 650 basis points to its fiscal 2027 annual recurring revenue (ARR) growth and, from fiscal 2028, at least 150 basis points a year to organic ARR growth.

Why it mattersUnderwriting is where an insurer decides which risks to take and at what price, so it is one of the highest-value places to apply AI. A core-systems vendor paying up to $2.1 billion for a specialist signals that AI pricing tools are moving from add-ons into the platforms carriers already run. For insurers, it raises questions about vendor lock-in, model governance and how regulators will view AI-assisted pricing decisions.
